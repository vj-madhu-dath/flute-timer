About Flute Timer

A quiet helper for the practice room.

Flute Timer began with a small annoyance: a two-handed instrument and a stopwatch that wanted a free hand. So the timer learned to listen instead.

There is a particular moment in a practice session when you finish a phrase, lower the flute, and wonder whether that was two minutes or five. A phone timer could tell you, but it is usually in your pocket, face down, behind a music stand, or in the hand that is holding the instrument.

Flute Timer is for that moment. It starts counting when sound appears, closes a lap when the sound stops, and keeps a simple record of the whole session. You do not press anything while you play.

The free-hand problem

Most practice timers are built like kitchen timers: set them, then leave them alone. That works until the instrument itself occupies both hands. A flutist cannot tap a lap button in the middle of a phrase, and probably should not want to.

Listening solves the interface problem. The app only needs to know two things: is there sustained sound right now, and has the sound been gone long enough to count as rest? Everything else follows from those two answers.

What it counts, and what it doesn't

The app measures loudness, not musicianship. Sound above a movable threshold opens a play lap. When the room stays quiet for a moment, that lap closes and a rest lap opens. The next sustained note begins another play lap.

That means it can count you playing long tones, a difficult passage, or a conversation with your teacher. It cannot tell whether the note was in tune, whether the rhythm was honest, or whether your attention wandered. Those are your work, or your teacher's.

This is a stopwatch with a session log, not a practice coach. The value is not in the number itself; it is in being able to look back and see the shape of the time you spent.

Privacy, in plain terms

The microphone stream is read by the browser's Web Audio API, reduced to a live level in decibels, and thrown away. No audio buffer is written to disk. Nothing is sent to a server. There is no account, no sign-in, and no tracking script.

Your settings — sensitivity, timing, and any room calibration — are kept in your browser's local storage. The current session's laps live only in the open tab. Closing or reloading the page clears the laps, while the settings remain for next time.

One honest limitation

A web page cannot promise that every browser and operating system handles microphone permissions the same way. Flute Timer asks only when you press Start, and it stops reading the microphone when you pause. If you want to inspect the code, it is all plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

Made for real rooms

A flute in a carpeted bedroom, a flute in a hall, and a flute beside an open window all sound different to a microphone. The timer gives you three adjustments for that: sensitivity, how long a note must last, and how long silence must last before it becomes rest. A two-second calibration can also learn the room's background level.

You normally do not need to touch any of them. The note on how the timer hears you explains what each control is doing when the automatic behavior is not quite right.

Questions, answered

Does it record my playing?

No. The app reads live loudness values from the microphone and discards the audio as it goes. Nothing is recorded, saved, uploaded, or sent anywhere.

Do I need an account or an app store?

No. Flute Timer is a static website. Open the timer page, allow the microphone, and start playing. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign into.

Will it work on my phone or tablet?

It should work in a current mobile browser. Microphone access requires a secure page, so use the hosted HTTPS site or open it on localhost from a computer. Put the device within reach of the instrument and give it a moment to calibrate.

Can it tell whether I played the right notes?

No. It measures amplitude, not pitch. It knows that sound is present and sustained; it does not know which note you played or whether it was in tune.

Why did one phrase split into two laps?

The gap in the middle was longer than the "silence before rest" setting. Raise that value in Fine tuning if short thinking pauses should stay inside the same play lap.

Can I use it with another instrument or voice?

Yes. Any sustained sound can open a play lap. Calibrate in the room you use, then adjust sensitivity if the timer is too eager or too sleepy.

What happens if I close the tab?

The current session's laps are cleared, because they only live in the open page. Your settings and room calibration are kept in local storage for the next session.

Why is it free?

Because there is no server to run. The whole site is a handful of static files. That also makes the privacy story simple: there is nowhere for audio to go.

Flute Timer is a small, deliberate tool. It is not a metronome, a tuner, a lesson, or a measure of how much you have improved. It is the clock you would have kept if you had a third hand — and it is happy to be ignored while you play.
